# Week 1: Building Your Script

## What you'll do this week

Write one solid ad body (the part of the script that doesn't change) and 15 to 20 hooks that swap in over the top of it. By the end of the week, you have a script ready to film.

## The lesson

### Why one body, many hooks

Most people try to write a new ad every time. New hook, new body, new everything. Then nothing performs and they have no idea why, because they changed five things at once.

We do it the opposite way. One solid body that we trust. 15 to 20 different hooks. We film all of them against that same body, then let the data tell us which hooks earned their spot. The hook is the only variable. Whichever ones move people, we keep. Whichever ones don't, we cut.

That is what "test one variable at a time" actually means in practice. Same body, different first 3 seconds, real numbers.

### What's in the script (the 6 parts)

Every ad has the same 6 parts in roughly this order:

1. **Hook.** The first 1 to 3 seconds. Whatever stops the scroll. A bold claim, a contradiction, a surprising line, a visually striking moment.
2. **Relatability.** A short beat where you tell her you were once exactly where she is. The specific struggle, the duration, the emotional cost, the moment it shifted. Goal: "she gets me, she was me."
3. **Content.** The actual teaching. The vehicle, the mechanism, why this works. This is the part that has to deliver real value, not vague hype.
4. **CTA 1.** The first clear next step. Calm, inviting, logical. "Click below to watch the free training."
5. **Twist the knife.** The cost of doing nothing. Honest, not manipulative. The "if you keep doing what you're doing, this is the version of next year that's waiting."
6. **CTA 2.** Same ask as CTA 1, but stronger because she's now emotionally bought in.

That is the spine. The body is everything from Relatability through Twist. The hook is the only piece we swap.

### Show, don't tell

This is the rule that quietly does the most work. A Meta video ad is a demonstration, not a monologue. Every time you say something important, you ask: how can I show this instead.

A few examples:

- Don't say "I built a business that runs without me." Show the dashboard. Show the laptop closed.
- Don't say "the old life." Show the scrubs, the parking lot, the alarm at 4:45am.
- Don't say "click the link below." Show the cursor moving to the button.
- Pattern interrupt every 3 to 4 seconds. New angle, new b-roll, new text overlay, anything that resets her attention.

When you write the script, leave a second column for what's on screen. Every line of voiceover gets a visual cue. We'll get into the actual filming in Week 2; the script you write this week is what we're filming from, so the SHOW notes go in now.

### Write the body first, hooks last

Most people want to write the hook first because it feels exciting. Wrong order. The body is the engine. The hook is the door. You can't pick the right door until you know what's behind it.

The order we use:

1. **Body + CTA first.** Get the Relatability, Content, CTA 1, Twist, and CTA 2 drafted. Read it out loud. Fix anything that sounds like a brand or a bot. The body should sound like you talking to one specific woman, not a marketing voice.
2. **ICA work.** Before you write a single hook, go back to your ICA notes. Who is she. What's her 2am thought. What stops her scroll. What makes her tune out instantly. Hooks are written FROM that, not at her.
3. **Hooks.** Open the hooks template (linked below). Write 15 to 20. Different angles, same audience.
4. **Narrow.** Read them out loud. Cut the ones that sound generic or that any coach on the internet could have written. Keep 10 to 20 that feel sharp.
5. **Film.** One filming session. Body once, every hook once. We'll cover the filming setup in Week 2.

### What makes a hook actually work

A hook isn't a slogan. It's a line that says "I see you" in the first 2 seconds. Three things keep showing up in the hooks that perform:

- **It names her reality.** Not "are you tired of the rat race." That's everyone. "Counting down to PTO is counting down your own life in small approved doses." That's her.
- **It's a statement, not a question.** "Ever wondered why..." is an AI tell and it doesn't land. Lead with something that actually says something.
- **It's specific.** Specific beats clever every time. The hook that names the exact moment she felt it (sitting in the car after a shift, the 2am scroll, the Sunday-night dread) is the hook that gets watched.

What to avoid: numbers ("how I made $X"), income claims of any kind, anything that sounds like "boss babe quit your 9 to 5." Meta is aggressive about this niche. We write hard without crossing that line, and that constraint is actually part of what makes our ads sound like us instead of like every other ad in her feed.

### A worked example: The Next Wave

The body we filmed for The Next Wave follows the same structure. The hook variations live in the [hook bank template](templates/hook-bank.md) with the framing we used. A few examples of what we wrote and why they passed our own filter:

- *"Hard work was never your problem. You have more of it than almost anyone."* Names her reality, opens by validating her, doesn't accuse her of anything (Meta-safe).
- *"A salary is rented income. The day you stop showing up, it stops showing up."* Statement, vehicle-focused, no income claim.
- *"I had the salary, the title, and the respect. I was also sitting in my car after shifts, completely empty."* First-person, specific image, Meta-safe (advertiser describing herself is always allowed).

What none of them do: promise a dollar amount, call out her job, use the word "easy," or try to sell anything in the first sentence. The hook earns 3 more seconds of her attention. That's the only job it has.

## Action items

By the end of this week:

1. **Confirm your ICA.** Open your ICA notes and read them out loud. If you can't picture one specific woman, fix that before you write anything else.
2. **Draft your body.** Use the [body script template](templates/body-script.md). Fill in Relatability, Content, CTA 1, Twist, CTA 2. Two columns: SAY and SHOW.
3. **Read the body out loud.** Time yourself. Target around 3 minutes 30 seconds of voiceover for the body, 5 minutes hard ceiling once a hook is on the front.
4. **Write 15 to 20 hooks** using the [hook bank template](templates/hook-bank.md). Different angles, same audience.
5. **Narrow to your top 15.** Read each one out loud. Cut anything that sounds generic.
6. **Hand it to me** (or your accountability partner) for a voice and compliance pass before you film.
