# Writing the Body

The body is the engine of the ad. It is everything from Relatability through CTA 2, and it is the part that does not change. You write it once, film it once, and every hook you test swaps in over the top of it. Get the body right and the only thing left to optimize is the first 3 seconds.

This page is how to write it. Use the [body script template](swipe-files/body-script.md) to actually draft it.

## Pick your format first

There are two shapes the body can take, and which one you write depends on how the ad asks her to respond:

- **Short-form (send message or instant form).** A tight version: hook, a quick relatability beat, one CTA to message you or fill a form, a short twist, a stronger CTA. Good when the next step is a conversation or a form, not a page. Runs well at 4:5.
- **Long-form edification (direct to webclass).** The full version this page walks through, where you tell the story, teach the mechanism, and point her to the host's free training. Two short edification beats name the host and why her hour is worth it. Written so those beats could be lifted out later if you ever host your own training. Runs well at 1:1.

The Next Wave runs the long-form version, so that is the model below. The beats are the same either way. Long-form just gives each one more room.

## Write the body first, hooks last

Most people want to write the hook first because it feels exciting. Wrong order. The body is the engine. The hook is the door. You cannot pick the right door until you know what is behind it.

The order we use:

1. **Body and CTA first.** Draft Relatability, Content, CTA 1, Twist, CTA 2. Read it out loud. Fix anything that sounds like a brand or a bot.
2. **Go back to your avatar.** Before you write a single hook, reread your [ICA](finding-your-ica.md) and [conversion quadrant](conversion-quadrant.md) notes.
3. **Then hooks.** 15 to 20 of them. (See [Hooks](hooks.md).)

## Two columns: SAY and SHOW

You write the body in two columns. What you SAY (voiceover or on camera) and what we SHOW (b-roll, screen recording, text overlay, an angle change). Every important line of SAY gets a SHOW cue. This is the [show, don't tell](foundations.md) rule turned into a format. You write the SHOW notes now because that is what you film from later.

## How to write each beat

### Relatability

She has to feel "she gets me, she was me" inside the first 30 seconds. This is your story, not a generic one. A specific moment, a specific feeling, the specific shift.

Lead with who you were and what you were doing, then the exact moment something cracked. First person the whole way. The more specific the image (the car after a shift, the Sunday-night dread, the alarm), the more real it feels.

SHOW: your old life. Scrubs, the work parking lot, the desk, the alarm clock. Pattern interrupt every 3 to 4 seconds.

### Content (the mechanism)

The teaching beat. Lead with the vehicle, not the income. Explain in plain language: where the product comes from, how the system works, why it does not require trading more hours. This is where you demonstrate.

Keep it tactile. Do not claim "I have a business that runs without me." Show the funnel, the dashboard, the loop, hands doing the thing. (More in [Creative and b-roll](creative-broll.md).)

SHOW: screen recordings, the funnel itself, simple visuals of how it runs.

### CTA 1

The first clear next step. Calm, inviting, logical. Say what the next thing is and show where the button is. Something like: "When you click below, you get the free training that walks through exactly how this works."

SHOW: the cursor moving to the button, the landing page.

### Twist the knife

The honest cost of doing nothing. Not manipulation, no fake urgency. Paint the version of next year that is waiting if nothing changes. Specific images: the same shifts, the same Sunday dread, the same mental math before every purchase.

This is the one beat that points at her future, not your past. Keep it true and let it sit.

SHOW: Sunday-night b-roll, the alarm, the parking lot again. Close on her future, not yours.

### CTA 2

Same ask as CTA 1, stronger now because she is bought in. This is where the emotional weight pays off into the click. "Click below. Watch the training. I'll see you on the other side."

SHOW: the final demonstration of the click. End on a clean closing frame.

## A worked example: The Next Wave

Here is how the beats came together for our own ad, so you can see the shape in real language. This is the model, not a script to copy. Yours has to sound like you.

- **Relatability:** the nurse story. The education, the ladder, the side things that all turned into more hours and less life. The moment of telling herself she should just be grateful, right up until she could not say it with a straight face.
- **Content:** the plain-language version of the vehicle. Partner with a company whose products people already want, you do not create or ship anything, a proven system does the explaining, set up an ad, the platform puts it in front of the right people, it keeps moving without being on the clock for 12 hours.
- **CTA 1:** point to the host's free training, under an hour, walks through exactly how it works.
- **Twist:** keep asking permission to take time off, keep doing the math before every purchase even though you make good money, keep watching other women build the life you want.
- **CTA 2:** consider this your sign, watch the free training, see you on the other side.

Notice what none of it does: no dollar figure, no income promise, no calling out her job in the second person, no "easy" or "passive." It still hits, because it is specific and true.

## Before you film

Run the checklist at the bottom of the [body script template](swipe-files/body-script.md). The big ones:

- Read the whole thing out loud. Does it sound like you talking to one woman, or like a brand talking to a market.
- Time it. Body voiceover under 4 minutes.
- Every SAY has a SHOW.
- No income claims, no dollar figures, no "easy" or "passive."
- No em dashes, no banned words.
- One clear next step (the free training).
