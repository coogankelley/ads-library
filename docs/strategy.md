# The strategy

Before anyone writes a hook or films a thing, the whole team needs to know the plan. Not "make some ads and see what happens." We run a deliberate test program: **one variable at a time, built off one script.** That is the entire game. Everything in this library serves it.

The reason is simple. If you change five things between two ads and one does better, you have learned nothing, because you do not know which of the five did it. So we hold everything still except the one thing we are testing. That is what lets the data actually mean something, and it is the difference between a system and throwing spaghetti at the wall hoping some sticks.

Here is the plan, start to finish.

## Step 0: pick your ad type

First you decide the kind of ad you are running. There is more than one way to build a video ad (long-form story-led, short-form, and others), and the type you pick is the container everything else gets tested inside. You lock it once, up front, and then it stays put while you test.

This is what the [Building your script](ad-anatomy.md) section is for. Start there to choose your format and learn the parts. Once your ad type is locked, you do not touch it again mid-test. It is the one thing we hold steady so everything below can move.

## Phase 1: test hooks first

The hook is the first 1 to 3 seconds, and it is the only piece we swap at this stage. So phase one is all about finding hooks that stop the scroll.

Here is how a round works:

1. **Lock one body you trust.** Write the body of the script once and leave it alone. This is the part that does not change. (See [Writing the body](writing-the-body.md).)
2. **Write 15 to 20 hooks.** Different angles, all on the same body. (See [Hooks](hooks.md).)
3. **Run one campaign, 15 to 20 ads, one hook each.** Same body, same audience, same everything. The hook is the only difference between them.
4. **Let the data pick.** Run them long enough to gather real numbers, then read the click-through rates. The hooks that clear the bar earned their spot. The ones well under are done.

Then you run it again. Keep your winner, cycle in fresh hooks, and run another round against the same body. We do this **3 to 5 times** until we have **3 to 5 hooks that reliably grab attention.** Those become your proven openers.

The mechanics of how a round actually gets read (the CTR scoreboard, how long to run, how to call a winner without calling it too early) live in [Testing](testing.md). This page is the plan. That page is the method.

## Phase 2: then test the body

Once you have your 3 to 5 winning hooks, the lever moves. Now you hold the hooks steady and start testing the body, still one variable at a time. The body is where there is a lot to test:

- **The relatability beat.** A different version of your story, told a different way.
- **The content.** How you teach the vehicle, what you show, the order you show it.
- **Where you filmed.** The setting, the backdrop, on camera versus b-roll.
- **The editing.** Pacing, captions, cuts, music.

Same rule as phase one: change one thing, run it, read the result, keep or kill. The discipline never changes. Only the lever you are pulling does.

## Why one thing at a time

It is tempting to fix everything at once, especially when an ad is not working. Resist it. The moment you change two variables together, you have blinded yourself to which one mattered. A clean test with one change teaches you more than a messy test with ten, every time. Slower on paper, faster in practice, because you are actually learning instead of guessing.
