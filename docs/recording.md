# Recording

Once your script is written and your hooks are checked, you film. One body, every hook, in one session if you can. This page is how to set up, what to read from, and how to deliver it so it never looks like you are reading.

## Set up once, film everything

The goal is a setup you can sit down at, film the body, then film every hook back to back without moving anything. Same framing, same light, same outfit, so they cut together cleanly later.

There is no single right rig. A phone, a way to hold it steady, and decent light will beat an expensive camera in a messy setup. Here is what actually matters.

**Film vertical.** We started out filming horizontal and cutting to a 1:1 square. The data moved. 4:5 and 16:9 are outperforming 1:1, so vertical is the better default now. Film vertical, and use your judgment on the final ratio for the specific ad you are posting. Either way, leave space around yourself in the frame so you can reframe and crop later without losing your head or your captions.

If you already have ads running in Meta on horizontal or 1:1, leave them. Do not re-cut what is working. This is the default going forward, not a reason to redo live ads.

**Get the camera to your eyeline.** The lens should sit level with your eyes, not below (makes you look down at her) and not above (makes you look small). This is the single biggest thing that separates a clean ad from an amateur one. Use a tripod and a stand to bring the phone up to your face.

**Light on your face.** Natural window light is free and flattering: face the window, not the other way. At night or in a dim room, one soft light in front of you does the job. Light goes in front of you, never behind, or you turn into a silhouette.

**Clean audio beats a nice camera.** A quiet room matters more than lens quality. A small clip-on mic beats a fancy camera with echo. Film away from hard empty walls and background noise. Bad audio makes an ad unwatchable no matter how good you look.

A simple kit you can leave set up:

- **A phone tripod** to hold the shot dead steady. [The one we use](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0F21SDXR2).
- **A stand to raise the camera to eyeline**, like a laptop stand or phone mount. [The one we use](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0D1N12YH5).
- **A clip-on mic**, optional but worth it, for clean audio. [The one we use](https://www.amazon.com/dp/B0FQBSMB4H).
- **Light:** a window, or one soft light placed in front of you.

## Reading without looking like you are reading

You are delivering a written script, but it can never look like it. The fix is a teleprompter, set up right.

**Use a teleprompter app.** It scrolls your script near the lens so your eyes stay on camera. A few that work well:

- **Teleprompter.com** (web, iOS, Android). Free tier, and the voice-activated scroll follows your pace and waits if you pause. Good default.
- **PromptSmart.** Its VoiceTrack scrolls as you speak and stops if you go off-script, so you never race the text.
- **BIGVU.** All-in-one if you want script help, auto-captions, and an eye-contact fix built in. More than you need for just a prompter, but handy.
- **Teleprompter Pro** (Apple). Free, no watermark, simple.

Voice-activated scrolling is the one feature worth caring about: it moves at your speed instead of forcing you to match a set crawl.

**Get the text as close to the lens as possible.** The closer the words are to the camera, the less your eyes move while reading. If your phone is the camera, the app overlays the script right at the lens. If you film on a separate camera, put the prompter phone directly under or beside the lens.

**Sit back from the camera.** Distance hides the read. Up close, anyone can see your eyes darting line to line. A few feet back, those small movements disappear and you just look like you are talking. Far enough that the reading is invisible.

**Read at the top, in chunks.** Glance and grab a phrase, deliver it to the lens, then glance for the next one. Read the lines as they appear at the top of the screen, not after they scroll to the middle, so your gaze stays high and near the camera. Do not scan word by word.

**You do not have to stare.** Locking onto the lens every second reads as robotic. Keep most of it on the lens, with natural glances away when you pause or land a point. Let your head and hands move like you would in a real conversation. Stiffness is what makes a read obvious, not the glances.

## Delivering it

You wrote it to one woman. Deliver it to her. Warm, direct, like you are talking across a table, not presenting to a room.

**Pace up.** Social ads need more energy than a normal talking-head video. Move a little quicker than feels natural, keep it tight, no long pauses for her thumb to wander off. Set your teleprompter scroll to that quicker pace so you are not waiting on the text.

**Time the body first.** Read it out loud and time it before you film. Body voiceover under 4 minutes. If it runs long on camera, it ran long on the page. Fix the page, not the take.

## Batch the hooks

The body is filmed once. The hooks are the variable you test, so film them all in the same session, against the same setup, so they cut onto the body seamlessly later.

- Film every hook back to back, same framing and outfit as the body.
- Get a couple of takes of each hook. Editing wants options, and the hook is the piece you swap most.
- Keep them labeled as you go, so you are not guessing which take is which in CapCut later.
