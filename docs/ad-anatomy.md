# Ad Anatomy

Every ad we run has the same 6 parts in roughly the same order. Once you can see the parts, you can write them, swap them, and diagnose what is broken. This page is the map. The next two pages ([Writing the body](writing-the-body.md) and [Hooks](hooks.md)) are where you actually build each piece.

## The 6 parts

**1. Hook.** The first 1 to 3 seconds. The only job is to stop the scroll and earn 3 more seconds of attention. A bold claim, a contradiction, a surprising line, a striking visual. This is the one piece we swap and test. More in [Hooks](hooks.md).

**2. Relatability.** A short beat where you tell her you were once exactly where she is. The specific struggle, how long it lasted, what it cost you, the moment it shifted. The goal is one feeling: "she gets me, she was me." This is your real story, first person.

**3. Content.** The actual teaching. The vehicle, how it works, why it is different. This is where you demonstrate. Show the funnel, the dashboard, the simple version of how it runs. This part has to deliver real value, not vague hype, or the rest does not land.

**4. CTA 1 (primary).** The first clear next step. Calm, inviting, logical. "When you click below, you get the free training that walks through exactly how this works." Demonstrate where the button is, do not just say it.

**5. Twist the knife.** The honest cost of doing nothing. Not manipulation, not fake urgency. Just the truthful picture of what next year looks like if nothing changes. Same shifts, same Sunday dread, same Monday math.

**6. CTA 2 (closer).** The same ask as CTA 1, stronger now because she is emotionally bought in. This is where the weight of the twist pays off into the click.

## Body vs hook

This is the most important distinction in how we build ads, so get it now:

- **The body** is everything from Relatability through CTA 2. You write it once. You film it once. It does not change.
- **The hook** is part 1 only. It is the one variable. You write 15 to 20 of them and film all of them against that same body.

Why we do it this way: if you change five things every time, you never learn what worked. By holding the body steady and only swapping the hook, the data can actually tell you which first-3-seconds earned their spot. Whichever hooks move people, you keep. The rest you cut. That is what "test one variable at a time" means in practice. (See [Testing](testing.md).)

## In our format: where the host beats go

We run a long-form, story-led ad that drives to the webclass. On top of the 6 parts, our version has two short edification beats where you point to the webclass host and why she is worth an hour. They slot in like this:

1. Hook
2. Relatability
3. Edification 1 (how the host showed you the way)
4. Content (the mechanism)
5. Transition + Edification 2 (why her free training explains it better than you can)
6. CTA 1
7. Twist the knife
8. CTA 2

We write these so the edification beats could be lifted out later without the script breaking. If you ever host your own training, the spine still stands.

## How the parts hand off

The parts are not islands. Each one sets up the next:

- The **hook** buys attention so she stays for the **relatability**.
- The **relatability** earns trust so she believes the **content**.
- The **content** delivers value so the **CTA** feels like a logical next step, not a sales jump.
- The **twist** raises the stakes so **CTA 2** lands harder than CTA 1 did.

When an ad feels off, it is almost always one handoff that is broken. Diagnose by part.

## Length

- Body voiceover: about 3 minutes 30 seconds.
- Total with a hook on the front: 5 minutes hard ceiling.
- That is roughly 480 to 525 words of voiceover for the body, at about 140 words a minute.
