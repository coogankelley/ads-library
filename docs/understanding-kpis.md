# Understanding your KPIs

A plain-language guide to what you track, what each number is telling you, and when it is time to act. Check it daily, anytime ads are live. It works for every ad type.

## Why KPIs run the whole thing

KPI is short for Key Performance Indicator: one specific, measurable number that shows how a single step of your funnel is actually performing.

Skip them and you are flying blind. You can see ads are running and leads are trickling in, but you have no way to tell where your funnel is pulling its weight, where it is bleeding, or what to fix first. KPIs take the guesswork out of it. Each number is the story of one stage, from the second someone sees your ad to the second they buy. The moment a KPI slips under its baseline, you know which stage broke and where to look.

You are not babysitting every number every day. The system is simpler: glance at the tracker daily, catch anything sitting below baseline, and know on sight what it means.

Your tracker holds two kinds of numbers:

- **Inputs** are the raw numbers you type in yourself: spend, leads, booked calls, closed deals.
- **Calculated** metrics are the percentages and costs the sheet works out for you from those inputs: CTR, conversion rates, cost per lead, and the rest.

Fill in the input columns and let the sheet handle everything else.

## Pick your tracker

There are two versions in your "ADS REFERENCES" folder. Grab the one that matches your campaign, then make your own copy in your Drive before you touch it. Never type over the master.

| Tracker | Use it for | What it watches |
|---|---|---|
| KPI Tracker, Landing Page (long form / direct to webinar) | Traffic going to a landing page or webinar registration | Leads, attendees, saw offer, and back-end sales |
| KPI Tracker, DM & Instant Form (short form / send message + instant form) | Send message and instant form campaigns | New DMs, connections, MQLs, pushed to webclass, and back-end sales |

## Baselines: your minimum standards

A baseline is the floor for each key step, not the dream number. It is the lowest a metric can sit before it is officially a problem. Anytime something drops under its baseline, it points you straight at a bottleneck. Treat a below-baseline number as a signal, not an emergency, and use the last column to know where to look.

| # | Metric | Floor | What it measures | If it is under the floor, look here |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 01 | CTR (All) | >6% | Share of impressions that get any click | The creative or hook. Either the spoken hook (your first line) isn't stopping the scroll, or the opening shot isn't grabbing the eye. |
| 02 | CTR (Link) | >3% | Share of impressions that click through to your page or funnel | The body or the CTA. Either the ad isn't proving enough value to keep them watching, or the call to action is weak. |
| 03 | Lead Conversion Rate | >20% | Share of landing page visitors who opt in or register | The page itself. The headline promise may be too soft, or the layout too cluttered. Direct-to-landing-page ads only. |
| 04 | Saw Offer Conversion Rate | >20% | Share who stayed in the webclass long enough to reach the offer | Retention and delivery. Early drop-off is a weak hook; mid-class is thin content; drop-off right before the pitch is a weak transition into the offer. |
| 05 | Front End Sale Conversion Rate | >3% | Share who saw the offer and bought the front-end product | Offer and pitch. The offer may feel too light for the price, or there isn't enough proof it works. |
| 06 | Booked Call Rate | >70% | Share of eligible front-end buyers who book a strategy call | Your Discovery Process, posture, or urgency. The process isn't building value for the call, or nothing makes them feel they need to book now. |
| 07 | Show Rate | >80% | Share of scheduled calls the prospect actually attends | Reminders and confirmation. Not enough posture around the call, or not enough reminders before it. |
| 08 | Close Rate (back-end sale) | >10% | Share of attended calls that turn into a back-end sale | Lead quality and sales skill. A pile of "I can't afford it" is a lead-quality issue upstream; more often it is a gap in discovery, objection handling, or the close. |

## Glossary and calculations

Every column in the tracker, defined. Anything marked **Calculated** is filled in by a formula, so you never type into it. You only enter the **Input** columns. A few inputs pull straight from Meta or GHL, noted where it applies.

| Term | Type | What it means | Formula |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spend | Input (Meta) | Total ad dollars spent in the period | n/a |
| CPM | Input (Meta) | Cost to reach 1,000 impressions (how pricey your audience is) | Spend ÷ (Impressions ÷ 1,000) |
| Impressions | Input (Meta) | Times your ad showed on screen (repeat views count) | n/a |
| Clicks (All) | Input (Meta) | Every click: link clicks, profile visits, likes, shares | n/a |
| Unique Link Clicks | Input (Meta) | Individual people who clicked your link at least once | n/a |
| CTR (All) | Calculated | Share of impressions that got any click | Clicks (All) ÷ Impressions |
| CTR (Link) | Calculated | Share of impressions that clicked through to your page | Link Clicks ÷ Impressions |
| Leads | Input | People who handed over their info (opt-in, survey, registration) | n/a |
| Lead Conv Rate | Calculated | Share of page visitors who became a lead | Leads ÷ Page Visits (or unique link clicks) |
| Cost Per Lead | Calculated | Average cost per lead | Spend ÷ Leads |
| New DMs | Input | New DM conversations from your ads (first messages only) | n/a |
| Cost Per DM | Calculated | Average cost per new DM conversation | Spend ÷ New DMs |
| DM Responses | Input | People who replied to your DM (first replies only) | n/a |
| My Response Rate | Calculated | Share of inbound DMs your team replied to | DM Responses ÷ New DMs |
| Connections | Input | People who moved into a genuine two-way conversation | n/a |
| Connection Rate | Calculated | Share of new DMs that became real conversations | Connections ÷ New DMs |
| Marketing Qualified Leads (MQLs) | Input | Leads who passed your qualification bar (fit, interest, readiness) | n/a |
| MQL Rate | Calculated | Share of new DMs that became MQLs | MQLs ÷ New DMs |
| Pushed to Webclass | Input | People moved into the webclass stage | n/a |
| Saw Offer / Completed Webclass | Input | People who stayed long enough to see the offer | n/a |
| Saw Offer Conv Rate | Calculated | Share who made it far enough to see the offer | Saw Offer ÷ Pushed to Webclass (or attendees) |
| Cost Per Saw Offer | Calculated | Average cost to get one person to the offer | Spend ÷ Saw Offer |
| Front End Sale | Input | Total front-end purchases through the funnel | n/a |
| Front End Sale Conv Rate | Calculated | Share who bought the front end | Front End Sales ÷ Leads |
| Cost Per Acquisition (CPA) | Calculated | Average spend to land one front-end sale | Spend ÷ Front End Sales |
| Completes Discovery Process | Input | Buyers who finished the full Discovery Process | n/a |
| Discovery Process Completion Rate | Calculated | Share of buyers who finished it | Completes Discovery ÷ Front End Sales |
| Booked Calls | Input | Total calls scheduled with prospects | n/a |
| Book Call Rate | Calculated | Share of eligible buyers who booked | Booked Calls ÷ Front End Sales |
| Cost Per Booked Call | Calculated | Average spend per booked call | Spend ÷ Booked Calls |
| Attended | Input | People who showed up to the booked call | n/a |
| Show Rate | Calculated | Share of booked calls actually attended | Attended ÷ Booked Calls |
| Closed | Input | Back-end deals won in the period | n/a |
| Close Rate | Calculated | Share of attended calls that closed | Closed ÷ Attended |
| Package | Input | Dollar value of the offer the customer bought | n/a |
| Commission | Input | Total commission earned from closed deals | n/a |
| Earnings Per Call | Calculated | Average commission per booked call (no-shows included) | Commission ÷ Booked Calls |
| Earnings Per Appointment | Calculated | Average commission per attended appointment | Commission ÷ Attended Calls |
