# Headlines and Primary Text

The video is the ad, but the headline and the primary text (the caption above the video) do real work too. Meta reads them, and so does she. The headline is the short line that shows under the video. The primary text is the caption that sits above it. Both get written from your [ICA](finding-your-ica.md), and both have to pass the same [compliance](compliance.md) sweep as the video.

## Headline Formulas

The headline is one line. Pick a formula, fill it with her real situation, keep it claim-free. Eight that work:

- **How to (what she wants) without (her big fear).** "How to Build Income Online Without a Product of Your Own."
- **The fastest way to (something she wants).** "The Fastest Way to Replace a 9-to-5 Income Without Starting From Scratch."
- **How I went from (undesired) to (desired).** "How I Went From Dreading Mondays to Working From My Laptop."
- **What nobody tells you about (her situation).** "What Nobody Tells You About Building Income While Working Full Time."
- **I used to (relatable situation). Here's what changed.** "I Used to Dread Sunday Nights. Here's What Changed."
- **For the (specific person) who (specific situation).** "For the Woman Who Has Done Everything Right and Still Feels Stuck."
- **The real reason (common belief). And what works instead.** "The Real Reason Your Side Hustle Isn't Working. And What to Do Instead."
- **The simplest way to (outcome) without (fear).** "The Simplest Way to Build Income Online Without a Following or Sales Calls."

Notice none of them carry a dollar figure or an income promise. "Replace a 9-to-5 income" describes a goal, not a result. Keep yours on that side of the line.

## Primary Text (the Caption)

It does not need to be long. It does need to call out your avatar so the right woman feels it is for her. Seven principles:

### 1. Open With a Scene

Start with a moment she recognizes, without asking her to admit anything. "I used to sit at my laptop mid-meeting thinking, wait, is this really my life?" One line like that does more than a paragraph of questions. The scene creates identification instantly. She does not have to raise her hand, she just feels seen.

### 2. Specificity Is the Targeting

Meta's algorithm finds your buyer through your creative. The more specific your copy, the more precisely it matches you to the right person. "A PTO request for a dentist appointment" outperforms "feeling stuck at work" every time. One real, specific detail beats three general pain points. Write for one person and Meta will find her.

### 3. Use the AND Structure

Hold two truths together: validate where she is AND expand what she thinks is possible. "You can be grateful for your job AND still want more freedom." It removes guilt before it becomes an objection, and it is what makes someone feel seen instead of sold to.

### 4. Reframe the Pain: System, Not Self

Do not leave her sitting in the pain. Move from "you're tired and stuck" to "you're not being dramatic, you're waking up." Always run the order: pain, then reframe, then possibility. That is the difference between a reader who feels bad and one who feels ready.

### 5. Earn the CTA Before You Ask for It

Cold traffic in 2026 has a finely tuned sales radar. If the CTA feels like the point of the whole caption, trust collapses. It should feel like the natural next step after real value. "No pressure. Just a chance to see what's possible." Earn the ask, then make it.

### 6. Write for One Person

The caption should sound like it was written for one specific woman, not broadcast to a crowd. Read it back and ask: does this sound like a message to my ideal client, or an announcement to a room?

### 7. White Space Is Conversion Design

Most of your traffic reads on mobile. Dense paragraphs get scrolled past before they are read. Short lines. A line break between every thought. The eye should move down the page in under ten seconds. The best copy in the world does not convert if nobody reads it.

## The CTA Line

End the caption by telling her what to do and what happens next. Match it to the next step in your funnel:

- **Send Message ads:** "Send me the word READY and I'll send you the free training."
- **Instant Form ads:** "Fill out the quick form so I can learn about your goals, then you get instant access to the training."
- **Direct to webclass:** "Tap the link and watch the free training."

## A Worked Caption (Claim-Free)

This is the primary text from a live Next Wave ad, so you can see the principles in one piece. Scene first, specific details, the AND structure, white space throughout, and the ask earned at the end:

> You don't want a different job. You want a different life.
>
> This is for the woman who's done measuring her year in time-off requests. Who's over spending her days off recovering instead of actually living them. Who scrolls past women traveling and breathing freely and quietly wonders when it's her turn.
>
> If that feels familiar, keep reading.
>
> You can crave stability AND still want to book a flight on a Tuesday. You can be ambitious AND still want slow mornings with no alarm. You can love parts of your life AND know you've outgrown the rest.
>
> The women living like that didn't get lucky. They changed one thing: how they earned.
>
> That's what this free training breaks down. No product to create, no following to build, no chasing friends and family. Our team walks you through the whole thing, step by step, in under an hour.
>
> No pressure. Just a chance to see what's possible. Tap the link and watch the free webclass.

No dollar figures, no income promise, no "you're exhausted" call-out of her job. It still lands, because it is specific and true.
